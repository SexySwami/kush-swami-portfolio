Headwinds we planned around.

External factors
Economic

With a recession looming, 62% of Canadians are eating out less and 46% are switching to cheaper brands. Droughts in Brazil and Colombia pushed coffee prices to multi-year highs.

Political

Boycott calls had already slowed Starbucks' sales in early 2024 — and 71% of Canadians want to know where their food comes from, so ethical sourcing has to be airtight.

Legal

Health Canada caps caffeine at 180 mg per serving, and caffeinated drinks can't be sold as sports electrolyte replacements — so the message stays on lifestyle, not performance claims.

✕

Product differentiation

Leading with protein, electrolytes and collagen turns the drink into a spec sheet — and "exercise performance coffee" doesn't give people a picture to hold onto.

✓

Lifestyle positioning

Show the drink inside an active, everyday life. It lets Starbucks start associating its brand with healthy living — reaching people who don't yet see it as a healthy option.

BioSteel

Wins with pro-athlete endorsements and team sponsorships, sold everywhere from gyms to grocery.

Vega

Wins on organic, non-GMO clean labels and content that teaches the benefits of plant-based nutrition.

Celsius

Wins with "burn fat, boost metabolism" claims, gym partnerships and fitness influencers.

Why $4.99

Shelf logic. Starbucks prices its Doubleshot Energy and Frappuccino cans identically ($3.79–$4.49) to compete side by side — Protein Coffee follows the same playbook.

Room at the top. Wellness buyers already pay premium — Athletic Greens sells for $100+ a bag — so under-pricing would undersell the brand.

A new market. Health is new ground for Starbucks; anything over $7–8 a can would scare off first-time buyers.

Pricing at $4.99 sits $1–2 above competitors — what customers will pay for the Starbucks name — while matching the brand's other canned drinks and staying well below the $7–8 ceiling for a single can.

07 — The big idea

A wall built
from encouragement.

Outside a high-traffic GoodLife Fitness in downtown Toronto — with a Starbucks nearby — we put up a Wall of Motivation. Passers-by, gym-goers and people deciding whether to go in leave sticky notes for each other. Together they make a rainbow of motivation.

Live wall · 10 notes
#ItsYourMove

The messages don't stay on the wall. They're printed on the cans and become the copy for every ad — so the people who joined the campaign literally write it.

No product sampling at the event: the wall, notes and materials carry the Starbucks logo and hashtag, keeping it about encouragement, not a sales pitch.

The benefit: position Protein Coffee as an accessible, motivating drink that slots into an active life — and use #ItsYourMove to build a community that lowers the barriers to getting started.

The support

"A little bit of protein to give you the strength to perform better at the gym — and a whole lot of energy from the coffee to get your butt in the gym."

Being tired is tied to lacking motivation. Coffee already solves tired — so it can sell motivation.

Emotional

Help people get past the barriers to exercise and feel empowered on their own terms.

Social

#ItsYourMove invites people to share the small ways they stay active — and lift others up.

Inspirational

Real people reaching their goals, with Starbucks Protein Coffee as a companion, not a coach.
